**Conclusion**: a custom order is not an upgraded version of the same item — it sells **your time and the risk you carry**. Charge in three layers, publish three rules, compute capacity first. Quoting as "standard price + 20%" is close to guaranteed loss.

## 1. Three pricing layers (structure; substitute your numbers)

```
custom price = floor price × custom factor (1.3–1.8)
             + (communication + sampling + revision) hours × your hourly rate
             + risk premium (rework and refund probability past the point of no return)
```

- **Floor price**: compute it with the formula in the [pricing guide](/guides/2026-10-30-pricing-handmade/). Do not reuse your retail price; retail carries flexibility that custom work does not.
- **Custom factor 1.3–1.8**: resizing 1.3, colour/material change 1.5, added name or bespoke design 1.8. Below 1.3 you are doing design work for free.
- **Communication and sampling hours are priced separately.** This is the hidden cost of custom work: ten message rounds plus one sample is half a day, and nobody pays you for that half day.
- **Risk premium**: add 10–15% for rework probability after the point of no return, and state in the rules that nothing is refundable past it.

One test: **if the buyer cancels, does the deposit already cover materials plus hours spent?** If not, the pricing structure is wrong.

## 2. Three rules (on the listing, and in the first message)

1. **50% non-refundable deposit**; raise the share when it does not cover materials plus communication.
2. **Revision rounds are finite**: two rounds included, each further round billed at your hourly rate. Define a round ("one consolidated round of feedback").
3. **Define the point of no return** — firing, glazing, engraving, cutting. Before it, changes are fine. After it, remedies only.

These three rules are not about defending against customers; they **reduce arguments**. The more specific the rule, the fewer disputes — and the faster deals close, because serious buyers feel safe reading them.

## 3. Kill half the communication cost with a form

Turn message rounds into **five required fields before quoting**:

1. Purpose and deadline (is there a hard date: birthday, wedding)
2. Size and colour (with a reference object)
3. Text content (case, font, placement)
4. Budget range
5. Acceptable material substitutions (your stance on shortage and natural colour variation)

Field 4 is the filter: **enquiries clearly below your cost get a polite no**, before quoting and sampling. Field 5 is your disclaimer; handmade has natural variation, and saying it upfront is cheaper than explaining it afterwards.

## 4. Capacity: compute the deadline backwards before you accept

```
capacity = pieces per month − scheduled − peak reserve (20%) − repeat-order reserve (10%)
last day to accept = delivery date − (making days + sampling/confirmation days + shipping days + 5-day buffer)
```

- In peak season (Q4, pre–Mother's Day) do not take "rush" jobs, only "early" ones. The rush premium never pays for a broken schedule.
- For hard-date orders (weddings, birthdays), **nothing enters production before the proof is confirmed**, and the rule "unconfirmed after X days means the date moves" is written down.

## 5. The judgement: custom is the highest form of human endowment, and the easiest to optimise away

> "Luxury never disappears — because what is luxurious is not efficiency but scarcity: the value a person endows."
> — from OJ's own notes (redacted)

Custom orders are where this line is hardest: **the buyer is paying for the part only you can do**. So the goal of a custom process is not to save yourself work; it is to deliver your judgement — the proofs, the material advice, the things you refused. Those belong in the messages and on the card in the parcel.

The classic failure is making custom work efficient by turning it into "pick a template". Once it is reproducible, the premium is zero, because reproducible things carry no scarcity.

## 6. Three things to do this week

1. Pull last month's custom orders, reprice them with the three layers, and count how many did not cover your communication hours.
2. Turn the five fields into a form; put it on the listing and in your auto-reply.
3. Publish the three rules (deposit, rounds, point of no return) in both languages, with both units.
