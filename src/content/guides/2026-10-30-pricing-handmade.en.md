**Conclusion**: pricing handmade work is not material times a multiplier. It is putting **everything you spend on this piece** into the number, then deciding whether it should be sold at all. What you leave out does not disappear; it becomes invisible loss.

## Six things that must be in the number

| Item | What gets forgotten |
|---|---|
| Material | Only the main material, not the small parts, packaging or waste |
| Labour | Only making, not sourcing, shooting, support, packing, shipping |
| Failure rate | Ten made, two ruined: those two belong in the cost of the eight |
| Platform fees | Commission, payment fees, ads, promotions |
| Shipping and returns | Free shipping is not free; one return often costs two shipments |
| Your time | If your hours are priced at zero, they stay at zero forever |

## A formula you can use today

```
floor = (material + small parts + packaging) / (1 - failure rate)
        + total hours x your hourly rate
        + shipping + platform fees + returns reserve
price = floor x your premium factor (1.5 - 3)
```

A structural example (replace the numbers): material 60, small parts and packaging 15, failure rate 20% → 94 after amortisation; 4 hours at 80 = 320; shipping and fees 40 → floor about 454; premium factor 2 → price about 900.

If you do not dare quote that number, either the hourly rate is too high for your current skill, or the product cannot carry the premium — **not daring to quote is usually a product problem, not a pricing problem**.

## Three judgements

1. **Set the price before you make the piece.** Deciding afterwards means you will rationalise the cost you already spent.
2. **The premium has to be sayable**: scarce material, non-replicable process, customisation, after-sales promise. Buyers pay for reasons they can read.
3. **Raise in steps**: 10–15% at a time, each with a note (new process, new packaging, new promise) so returning buyers read it as an upgrade.

> "Luxury never disappears — because what is luxurious is not efficiency but scarcity: the value a person endows."

Handmade pricing was never cost plus margin. It is what you endow. Counting cost tells you the floor; saying what you endow is how you reach the ceiling.
