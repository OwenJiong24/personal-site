**Conclusion**: a listing converts in three stages, **in a fixed order** — ① found (attributes + title) ② clicked (main photo) ③ trusted (sizes + use + policies). Most sellers spend all their time on the description in stage three, which is the last thing to take effect.

## Stage 1 — being found: attributes outrank your prose

Platforms and search engines read your **structured fields** (attributes, tags, category, material, colour, occasion), not your adjectives.

- Fill every field you are allowed to fill. Attributes are filters: leave one blank and you delete one route to being found.
- Title formula: **category + material + occasion/recipient + one differentiator**. Example: `handmade ceramic mug | matte glaze | desk-alone-time | personalisable name`
- The first 40 characters decide, because most placements truncate around there.
- Keep internal SKU codes, series names and private abbreviations out of the title.

> "Google Analytics is essentially a tag… satisfy the structured-data requirement and over 90% of external services become available (Hotjar, Pinterest, ad channels) — they all need a built-in tag."
> — from OJ's 2026 product notes (redacted)

The same line applies to a craft listing: **structured fields are the entry point**. Every attribute you fill is a readable door you hand to the platform's ranking system, to search engines and to AI answer engines. Beautiful prose with empty fields is invisible.

## Stage 2 — being clicked: the photo's job is legibility

The main photo does not have to be beautiful; it has to be **understandable among a wall of other items**. Rules are in [listing-ready photos on zero budget](/guides/2026-10-30-phone-photos/): 45 degree full view, clean background, same height and distance, no text overlay.

One addition: **the photo must show the use** (a hand on the mug, the necklace worn, something inside the bag). An item whose purpose you cannot read gets skipped in a feed.

## Stage 3 — being trusted: the description answers five questions

Do not write mood. Answer these, one paragraph each:

1. **What it is**: category, material, process — all in one sentence, do not hide it.
2. **How big**: give both units (cm and in) and one reference object ("fits a 500 ml bottle").
3. **How to use it / how not to**: dishwasher safe? sun safe? boiling water? These decide your return rate.
4. **Why this price**: hours, material origin, customisation, limited run. This is where premium lands — pair it with the [pricing guide](/guides/2026-10-30-pricing-handmade/).
5. **Shipping and returns**: dispatch window, shipping cost (see the [shipping maths guide](/guides/2026-11-01-shipping-math/)), and the custom-order no-return note.

**Size and units are the number one cause of international returns.** `12 cm / 4.7 in (rim diameter)` beats "about 12 cm".

## Four common mistakes

1. Adjective stacking ("gorgeous, luxe, aesthetic"): matches no query and builds no trust.
2. Copying a competitor's description: the fastest way to look identical, and both the platform and the buyer notice.
3. Empty attributes: you are voluntarily deleting a route to being found.
4. No FAQ: the three questions buyers always ask (size, care, lead time) become three messages and one return.

## A 20-minute rewrite you can copy

| Step | Do | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write five facts about this piece: material, size, hours, hardest part, who it is for | 5 |
| 2 | Rewrite the title with the formula in stage 1; fill every attribute field | 5 |
| 3 | Write the description as the five answers; delete every adjective | 7 |
| 4 | Add three FAQ lines (size, care, lead time) with both units | 3 |

Then change **one variable at a time** (title and attributes first, not the photo too), and watch two weeks of impressions, clicks and orders. Those three numbers tell you which stage was actually broken.
