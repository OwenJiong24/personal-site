**Conclusion**: sellable photos are not about gear. They are four things done in order — **window light, background, fixed camera height, one consistent edit**. A phone is enough.

## 1. Light (half the outcome)

- Find a **north-facing window** (no direct sun) and shoot during the day. Overcast beats sunny.
- Place the piece about a metre from the window so light comes from the side; side light gives form.
- If the shadow side is too dark, stand a sheet of white paper opposite as a bounce.
- **Do not use the ceiling light**: wrong colour temperature, hard shadows.

## 2. Background and camera position

- Background: white paper or plain cloth, clean and unwrinkled.
- Camera: fix the phone at one height (a stack of books works) and keep **the same height and distance for every shot**.
- Framing: the piece fills about 70% of the frame with breathing room around it.

## 3. The five shots you must have

1. **45 degree full view** — what it is, at a glance.
2. **Straight-on view** — the clean frame most marketplaces use as hero.
3. **Detail** — texture, stitching, glaze, engraving.
4. **Scale** — next to a coin, a hand, a familiar object.
5. **In use** — on a table, worn, in a bag; keep the background clean.

## 4. Edit in 30 seconds

- Change three things only: exposure slightly, white balance until the paper reads white, contrast a touch up.
- No filters, no skin smoothing, no text stickers.
- Apply the same preset to the whole set so it reads as one family.

## 5. Three hard rules for the hero image

1. Clean background, centred subject, no dead space.
2. No text or watermark on the main image; most marketplaces down-rank or crop them.
3. The first two images must match in style — buyers swipe.

## One check that keeps you honest

After shooting, ask three questions: **what is it, how big, why is it worth the price.** If three images answer them, it is ready to list.
