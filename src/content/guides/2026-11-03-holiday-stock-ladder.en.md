**The one-line conclusion**: holiday stock is a division, not a wish. Take the weekly rate you can **prove today**, multiply it by the number of selling weeks you can **absorb** before the shelf closes, subtract what you already hold. If you cannot prove a weekly rate, buy materials for two weeks of work.

The 2026 calendar, so the arithmetic has a shape: Thanksgiving **2026-11-26**, Black Friday **11-27**, Small Business Saturday **11-28**, Cyber Monday **11-30**, Hanukkah sundown **12-04** through **12-12**, Christmas **12-25**, Kwanzaa **12-26**.

## What we can prove, measured 2026-11-03

We sell one product on our own marketplace. Measured on 2026-11-03: **287 units** shown in stock, price **54.99 USD** against a list price of 89.99, and **0 reviews**.

We do not have a weekly sales rate for that product to publish. We are saying it plainly because the whole method below depends on which number you can defend, and "we have none yet" is a legitimate answer - it changes what you should buy.

**Two-week rule when you have no rate**: make only what two weeks of your own labour can cover, sell it, and let the first ten orders become the rate. For a maker working alone, that is usually 10-25 units, not 300.

## The ladder: selling weeks you can absorb

Work backwards from the last day you can ship and still arrive. For our own shop the practical window on the 2026 calendar is: 2026-11-03 → 2026-12-18 (about 6.5 weeks) for a made-to-order maker who stops taking Christmas orders when transit stops being reliable.

Weekly rate you can prove → weeks to sell down 287 units:

| Weekly rate | Weeks to clear 287 units | Clears before 2026-12-18? |
|---|---|---|
| 2 units/week | 144 | no |
| 5 units/week | 57 | no |
| 10 units/week | 29 | no |
| 20 units/week | 14 | no |
| 40 units/week | 7 | yes, just barely |

Read it as a diagnosis, not a verdict. **If 40 units a week is not a rate you have ever produced, 287 units is not stock, it is a January project.** The same table on your own numbers takes two minutes; that is the point of publishing ours.

## The stop rule, so you learn in January and not next October

After the last shipping day plus three weeks, check what you still hold:

- over-bought ≈ stock on hand - (weekly rate x selling weeks)
- if you are holding more than half of what you built for the season, the plan failed in purchasing, not in marketing.

Run it on our number: 287 held, 6.5 selling weeks. Break-even on the stop rule needs a rate near 44 units/week. We have never produced that. So by the rule we wrote for ourselves, **that listing is over-bought today** - we are publishing that before the season, so we cannot explain it away after it.

## The margin question you must answer before you buy materials

Stock only makes sense if it sells at a price that survives the fee, the shipping and the return. Three numbers come first, in this order:

1. **Break-even, by division.** `cost base / (1 - fee)`. The fee is not an added line; on a 518.25 cost base at a 13% example fee, the answer is 595.69, not 585.62. [Pricing guide](/guides/2026-10-30-pricing-handmade/), [pricing calculator](/tools/pricing-calculator/).
2. **Free-shipping threshold.** Holiday buyers arrive with shipping on their mind. `shipping / price` decides whether you charge it plainly or bake it in, and baking requires `(price + shipping + reserve) / (1 - fee)`. [Shipping guide](/guides/2026-11-01-shipping-math/), [shipping calculator](/tools/shipping-calculator/).
3. **What a discount costs you, per unit.** Decide the Cyber Monday question before you own the inventory. If the product only works at 20% off, the price is wrong, not the calendar.

## Custom orders change the shape

A custom order is inventory you do not have to pre-build, and it has its own arithmetic (deposit, minimum, hours that slip). See [custom order math](/guides/2026-11-02-custom-order-math/) before you promise a season of personalised work.
