## Conclusion: a shop's keystone price sets your wholesale price, and your margin is whatever survives

**When a store asks for wholesale, they are not asking for a discount - they are asking you to fit inside a doubling.** Keystone pricing means the shop sells at twice what it paid, so if you want your product on that shelf at $54.99, your wholesale price is at most $27.50. On an $18 cost that is a **34.5% margin, not the 50%** most makers quote from memory. The arithmetic below is done on our own published price and a worked cost; nothing here is an industry average.

## The three numbers that decide it

| Number | Where it comes from | Our worked example |
|---|---|---|
| Your direct retail price | what you actually charge today | **$54.99** (our listing, read 2026-11-03) |
| The shop's keystone retail | what the shop will show, at 2x its cost | must stay **near $54.99**, so at most **$54.99** |
| Your wholesale price | keystone retail ÷ 2 | **$27.50** |
| Your cost per unit | materials + labour + packaging | **$18.00** (worked figure; our packaging line is $0.85-$2.60 per order, measured 2026-11-10) |
| **Your margin at wholesale** | (wholesale - cost) ÷ wholesale | **($27.50 - $18.00) ÷ $27.50 = 34.5%** |

**34.5%, not 50%.** That gap is the whole page. A maker who quotes "50% wholesale" from a $54.99 retail believes they are offering $27.50 at a 50% margin; they are offering it at 34.5%, and $9.50 of every unit goes to material and labour with $9.50 left over.

## Why "50% off" and "50% margin" are different animals

This is the same division trap that breaks fee arithmetic, and it bites harder in wholesale because two people are doing it at once:

- **Discount view**: $54.99 retail, 50% off → **$27.50**. Sounds like a 50% cut of the *revenue*, which it is.
- **Margin view**: to earn a 50% margin at $27.50, cost must be **$13.75** ($27.50 × 0.5). At $18 cost you earn **34.5%**.

| Wholesale price | Cost $18 | Margin | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| $36.00 | 50.0% | healthy | but keystone retail becomes $72 - above your own $54.99, so you undercut your own channel |
| $30.00 | 40.0% | thin but real | keystone retail $60, still above your $54.99 |
| **$27.50** | **34.5%** | **the keystone match** | retail $55, level with your own price - this is the real wholesale quote |
| $24.00 | 25.0% | below most makers' break-even | retail $48, cheaper than your own site - you are now competing with your own customer |

**Read the last row.** Wholesaling at $24 does not just thin your margin - it puts a store on the market selling your product for $48 while your own site asks $54.99. You have created your own competitor, and every direct sale now looks overpriced.

## The five-line check before you say a number

1. Write your **direct retail price** and the date you last verified it.
2. Write your **true cost per unit** including packaging (our range: $0.85-$2.60 per order).
3. Compute **keystone wholesale = retail ÷ 2**.
4. Compute **margin = (keystone wholesale − cost) ÷ keystone wholesale**.
5. If that margin is under your break-even, **do not take the order** - fix cost or retail instead.

Break-even, for reference, is the point where a unit's contribution covers fixed costs: if your fixed costs are $300 a month and each unit contributes $9.50 at wholesale, you need **32 wholesale units a month** just to stand still.

## What wholesale actually costs you beyond the margin

- **Packaging changes**: wholesale units ship in cases, not padded mailers, so the per-unit packing cost usually drops - but you buy cases and pay freight, which the margin above does not include.
- **Payment terms**: a shop may ask for net-30 or net-60. A $27.50 unit paid 60 days late is not the same $27.50. At a 5% cost of capital that delay costs about **$0.23 per unit** - small per unit, real per order.
- **Returns and damages**: retail buyers return to the shop; the shop returns to you. Budget the damage rate you already measured (our breakage page's table starts at 1%).
- **Your own channel's price floor**: once a shop shows your product at $48, you cannot easily raise your direct price without stranding them.
