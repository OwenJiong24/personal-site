## Conclusion: the fee table fools you on the base, not on the percentage

**A column of percentages tells you nothing.** Same item measured on 2026-11-04: selling price **$50**, shipping charged to the buyer **$8** (so $58 changes hands), materials **$18**, labour **1.5 hours at $20** - cost **$48**. Every number below was read from the platform's own page that day, with the URL.

Same $50 item, published rates from 2.9% to 13.6%, and the largest gap in what lands in your account is **$5.31** (Shopify's $47.02 against eBay's $41.71). **All four are losses here**, because a $48 cost with a $50 price has no room in it. **A fee table cannot rescue a price that was never going to work.**

## The three traps in every fee comparison

**1. Commission is division, not addition.** To keep $40 in your pocket at a combined 16.5% (13.6% + 2.9%), the price is 40 ÷ (1 − 0.165) = **$47.90**. "40 plus 16.5%" gives **$46.60** - you under-price by **$1.30 on every order**, which is **$130 a month** at 100 orders. Our [pricing calculator](/tools/pricing-calculator/) implements the division and prints the formula on the page.

**2. The base includes shipping, and sometimes tax.** From eBay's own page on 2026-11-04: a $150 item, $50 shipping, $10.50 tax, buyer pays $210.50; the fee is 13.6% of 210.50 = **$28.63**, plus $0.40 = **$29.03**. Etsy's 6.5% covers shipping and gift wrapping, and US shops are not charged it on sales tax; outside the US the tax sits inside the displayed price, so the fee applies to it too. **The heavier your shipping, the more the platform taxes it.**

**3. Ad fees fire on attributed orders only - do not spread them over everything.** Etsy Offsite Ads: **15%**, dropping to **12%** past $10,000 in 365 days, capped at **$100** per order, charged only when a click on an offsite ad precedes an order within 30 days. Spread as a flat 15% it prices every order for the worst case and you lose conversions; ignored entirely, you are gambling that you never need traffic. Our recommendation: price as if attributed, then decide whether to opt out.

## Verify it yourself in two minutes

1. Open **one real order in your own dashboard** and copy every fee line (Etsy: Payment Account; eBay: Seller Hub order fees; Shopify: order details; ZART: Artist Center billing);
2. Compare it against the official page - **the page states rules, the bill states your category, your country and your plan**;
3. When they disagree, trust the bill and write the gap into your pricing sheet (category differences are the usual cause);
4. Re-read the pages on the first of each month - fee changes usually land at month start. This site runs a weekly fee re-fetch; next alignment **2026-11-11**.
