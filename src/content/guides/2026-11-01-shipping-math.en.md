**Conclusion**: shipping has exactly three shapes — **bake it into the price (show "free shipping")**, **charge it plainly**, **set a free-shipping threshold**. The choice is one number: **shipping as a share of the price**.

## The ratio decides

| Shipping ÷ price | Buyer experience | Recommended shape |
|---|---|---|
| above 15% | shipping looks bigger than the item; checkout abandonment is worst here | bake it in, show free shipping |
| 8–15% | noticeable but tolerable | threshold shipping (section 3) |
| below 8% | barely noticed | charge plainly, keep the displayed price low |

Example (replace the numbers): price 90, shipping 6 → 6.7% → charge plainly, the displayed price stays low. Price 18, shipping 6 → 33% → bake it in, display 24 with free shipping.

## 1. Bake it into the price ("free shipping")

The cost does not disappear; it moves from the shipping line into the product line.

- Arithmetic: `shipped price = floor price + shipping + returns reserve`, then apply the premium factor from the [pricing guide](/guides/2026-10-30-pricing-handmade/).
- Upside: no second hit at checkout, steadier conversion, and you clear every "free shipping over X" promotion bar by default.
- Cost: **the displayed price rises**, so you look expensive in comparison contexts (search results, side-by-side listings). Returns tend to rise slightly, because a higher displayed price raises expectations.

## 2. Charge it plainly

- Upside: lower displayed price wins search and comparison; transparent cost; heavy parcels do not quietly lose money.
- Cost: checkout abandonment. Mitigation: put shipping on the **product page** ("shipping $6, ships within 48 hours"), never let the payment page be the first place they learn it.
- Fits: low-priced categories where shipping is a small share (stickers, small jewellery, material kits).

## 3. Threshold shipping (the right answer for most shops)

Set the threshold as **average order value × 1.2–1.5**. Below average order value you are just giving shipping away; above 1.5× nobody reaches it.

- Pair it with a live message on the product page and cart: "add $X for free shipping". This is the only switch that raises order value and conversion at the same time.
- Check it: the order at the threshold price must still be profitable. Verify with the [pricing calculator](/tools/pricing-calculator/).

## 4. Three details that eat the shipping margin

1. **Dimensional weight (DIM)**: light, bulky parcels bill on volume. Changing the box beats negotiating a carrier discount.
2. **Counting outbound only**: one return really costs about two legs plus packaging loss plus re-packing labour. With a monthly return rate r, add `r × 2 × one-way shipping` to every unit.
3. **Peak surcharges and rate rises**: carrier prices move within a year. Recompute your flat rate from the last 50 orders each quarter; do not ship 2025 numbers into 2026.

## 5. The judgement: shipping shape is a channel decision, not a price decision

> "In essence we gather people with the same need together, so we do not have to advertise to find orders — the orders find us."
> — from OJ's own notes (redacted)

Applied to shipping: **inside someone else's algorithm, follow its comparison rules** (free shipping, thresholds, total price including shipping). **On your own site, state the cost plainly** (charged, transparent, checkable). Using one shipping policy in both places is usually the wrong answer.

## 6. Three steps this week

1. Export your last 50 orders; compute the median shipping cost and the average order value.
2. Pick the shape from the table in section 1; set the threshold from section 3.
3. After changing, hold it for four weeks and record three numbers (visits, orders, revenue) so the next change has evidence.
