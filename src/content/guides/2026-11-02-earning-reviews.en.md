**Conclusion**: reviews are not begged for. They come from three moves — **shrink the gap** (real photos, dual units, honest lead times), **one specific ask in the parcel**, **one follow-up 7–14 days after delivery**. Paying for reviews is the most expensive option, because it buys away the single most useful piece of information you can get.

## 1. The gap is the biggest source of reviews

Buyers write when reality differs from expectation. Both directions write: delight writes, and disappointment writes harder. So review management starts with the listing, not the ask.

| Gap source | How the listing closes it |
|---|---|
| Size | dual units (cm + in) plus a reference object ("fits a 500 ml bottle") |
| Colour | state batch/kiln variation, show one natural-light photo |
| Lead time | write "customs take 15–20 days", never "ships in 3 days" to lift conversion |
| Texture | close-up detail shot (glaze, stitching, grain), not only mood shots |
| Packaging | show how fragile items are packed; arrival condition decides the first review |

This table, [the order a listing converts in](/guides/2026-11-01-listing-that-converts/), [shipping maths](/guides/2026-11-01-shipping-math/) and [custom order maths](/guides/2026-11-02-custom-order-math/) are the same discipline from three sides: **the more specific your page, the fewer messages, the steadier your reviews**.

## 2. The parcel card: one ask, one specific question

"Please leave a review" cards mostly do nothing. The working version makes the review easy to write:

```
Thank you for supporting a handmade shop.
If you feel like writing a review, answering one question is enough:
  Is the rim diameter bigger or smaller than you expected?
(If anything is off, reply to this note first — I will fix it.)
```

Three design points:
1. **One question** cuts the cost of writing a review from three minutes to thirty seconds.
2. The question targets **information the next buyer needs** (size feel), which is also what platforms rank reviews on.
3. **An exit ramp** ("contact me first") turns a public bad review into a private ticket you can solve before it becomes visible.

## 3. Follow-up rhythm: once, on day 7–14

- Timing: 7–14 days after delivery, when the object has been used.
- Frequency: once. One message is discipline, not efficiency.
- Channel: the platform message thread first (it sits next to the review button); email follows the opt-in rules from [moving buyers off the platform](/guides/2026-10-30-own-your-audience/).
- Structure: same as the card — one question, one exit.

**Measure your own baseline**: over the next 100 orders log three columns — asks sent, reviews received, reviews containing usable information. I have no checkable public conversion figures, so this page gives you a method, not a number. Any "industry average response rate" you read elsewhere is **unverified** and should not be imported.

## 4. Four things not to do

1. **Incentivised reviews**: coupons, cash back, refunds for five stars. Compliance risk, and it destroys the signal.
2. **Review pods, fake orders, friends mass-buying**: the pattern platforms detect first, and the penalty lands on the whole shop.
3. **Pressuring the unhappy buyer** (threats, repeated deletion requests): the screenshot becomes public, and the second injury is worse.
4. **Mass-inviting only happy buyers**: selective solicitation is restricted on most platforms; check your terms.

## 5. Bad-review SOP: turn it into information

1. **Public reply within 24 hours**: acknowledge, give the remedy, state what changed. The reply is written for the next buyer, not the reviewer.
2. **One private remedy**: replacement, refund, extra work — capped by your cost limit.
3. **Tag the cause** in a running table: size, colour, lead time, damage, expectation gap.
4. **Quarterly review**: the three most frequent causes go into the listing FAQ and the photo set. This step is the only return a bad review can produce.

## 6. Reviews are structured data too

> "Google Analytics is essentially a tag… satisfy the structured-data requirement and over 90% of external services become available (Hotjar, Pinterest, ad channels) — they all need a built-in tag."
> — from OJ's 2026 product notes (redacted)

For a maker: **a long review is reusable structured material**. Take the three most informative lines (size feel, durability, gifting context) and turn them into FAQ entries on your own product page. Published as FAQ they keep working; left inside a platform page they sink. Reviews, FAQ and process photos together answer the one question a stranger has: **can I check what you claim?**

> "Luxury never disappears — because what is luxurious is not efficiency but scarcity: the value a person endows."
> — from OJ's own notes (redacted)

Reviews matter more in handmade than in commodity goods, because the buyer is purchasing the endowment: **they want confirmation that someone else has already confirmed it is real.**

## 7. Three things this week

1. Audit each listing against the table in section 1: dual units, colour variation, honest lead time.
2. Make one card with a single question and an exit ramp; ship it with the next 20 orders.
3. Start the bad-review cause table, and tag every review from now on.
