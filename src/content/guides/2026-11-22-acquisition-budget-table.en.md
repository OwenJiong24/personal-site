## Conclusion: your acquisition ceiling is 24.41 dollars on this cost base, not a percentage of the price

**Your acquisition ceiling is 24.41 dollars on this cost base - the first-order contribution, not a share of price.** A measured 20% repeat rate lifts it to 30.51. Above that you are paying to lose money.

## The decision table

Inputs: contribution **$24.41** per order from the [pricing calculator example](/tools/pricing-calculator/); lifetime value from the [repeat-purchase arithmetic](/guides/repeat-purchase-math/). Acquisition cost is the amount you pay to get one customer.

| Acquisition cost | First order only | With a measured 20% repeat rate | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10 | Covered ($14.41 left) | Covered ($20.51 left) | **Go**, if the channel is real |
| $24.41 | Exactly break-even | Covered ($6.10 left) | **Borderline** — one return erases it |
| $30 | Loss of $5.59 | Covered ($0.51 left) | Only with a **measured** repeat rate |
| $50 | Loss of $25.59 | Loss of $19.49 | **No** — no rate on this base gets there |

**The boundary is the first-order contribution, not the price.** A channel charging $30 looks affordable next to an $82.78 product, but the product's contribution is 24.41, so the channel loses $5.59 before anyone comes back.

## The three channels, priced the same way

| Channel | What you actually pay | How to put it in the table |
|---|---|---|
| Paid ads | Invoice per click or per order | Divide spend by new customers; that is acquisition cost |
| Marketplace listing | Platform fees and less customer control | Fees already sit in the contribution; the relationship cost is not priced here |
| Own website + content | Your time, not an invoice | Record the hours and value them at the rate you use in the calculator |

**"Free" traffic is not free.** A marketplace listing removes the ad invoice but takes fees on every order and controls the customer relationship; a website removes the marketplace fee but consumes the hours you priced into the cost base. There is no row where acquisition costs zero unless you write your own time as zero, and we do not recommend that.

## Measuring acquisition cost without an ad invoice

If you have never bought an ad, acquisition cost is not zero — it is the time you spent making the listing, taking the photographs and writing the description, valued at the hourly rate you already put in your pricing calculator. Write down the hours once, before the campaign, not after it worked. If you do buy traffic, divide the invoice by **new customers**, not by clicks and not by orders from people who would have found you anyway. A channel that sends 200 clicks and 2 first-time buyers at a 60-dollar cost has an acquisition cost of 30 dollars per customer, whatever the click price says. The table above then tells you whether 30 dollars is survivable on your contribution. This is the one number sellers most often guess, and guessing it is how a profitable product turns into an expensive hobby.

## How to use the table this week

1. Compute contribution with your own numbers — price, materials, hours, fees, fixed charges. Do not use our 24.41.
2. Count repeat purchases in a fixed window. Six orders or twelve months; write the window down.
3. Find the acquisition cost of one channel: spend divided by new customers, not by clicks.
4. Look up the row. If it says no, do not run the campaign and hope the repeat rate saves it.
