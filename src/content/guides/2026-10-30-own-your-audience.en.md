**Conclusion**: only three things are portable away from a platform — **the email list, your own site, and the memory of you**. Everything else is rented.

## Why it matters

The platform's recommendation system decides who sees you; the only thing you control is whether they come back. A list is the one channel no algorithm governs: throttling, fee changes and shop suspensions do not reach it.

> "In essence we gather people with the same need together, so we do not have to advertise to find orders — the orders find us."
> — from my own notes (redacted)

Turn it around: **the list is your own demand-aggregation point.** You stop re-buying the same attention every month.

## Four steps, finishable in a month

### Step 1 — a domain mailbox that receives mail
Buy a domain, enable Email Routing, forward `hello@yourdomain` into your existing inbox. About US$10 a year.

### Step 2 — one subscribe entry point
- A form on your own site; ask for the email only.
- A card in every parcel: QR code plus one reason ("new work goes to this list first").
- A link in your shop profile, within the platform's external-link rules.

### Step 3 — a welcome email
Send it immediately: who you are, what you make, how often you will write, roughly when the next piece lands. **This email has the highest open rate your list will ever have.** Do not waste it.

### Step 4 — a fixed rhythm
Two to four sends a month, three kinds of content only: previews, process detail, restocks and limited runs. Unsubscribe link every time.

## Three traps

1. **Promotion only.** The list dies. Promotion is spending; content is investing.
2. **Buying a list.** Numbers look good briefly, then the spam folder, and it is illegal in many places.
3. **Sending too much.** Better to send less than to become one more email to delete.

## A checkable goal

Within three months: 100 subscribers, welcome-email open rate above 50%, and the first repeat purchase coming from someone on the list. Hit those three and you have a second leg that does not depend on the platform.
