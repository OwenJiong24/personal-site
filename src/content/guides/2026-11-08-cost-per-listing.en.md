## Conclusion: price your catalogue by what sells, not by what is up

**The commission is visible; the dead catalogue is silent, and it is usually bigger.** A listing costs money before any buyer appears: $0.20 on Etsy, expiring every four months and auto-renewing at $0.20 unless you turn it off; $0.35 past 250 monthly listings on eBay; $0 on Shopify and on our own marketplace, but $29 a month sits behind Shopify's plan. All rates below were read from the official pages on **2026-11-04**. This page is arithmetic on published rules, not a forecast.

## What one listing costs per year (published rules, 2026-11-04)

| Route | Per-listing charge | Renewal behaviour | One listing, one year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etsy | $0.20 | Expires after 4 months, auto-renews at $0.20 | **$0.80** with auto-renew on (publish + 3 renewals), **$0.20** off |
| eBay | $0.00 within 250/month, then $0.35 | Monthly allowance resets | **$0.00** small shop, **$0.35** above the allowance |
| Shopify | $0.00 | No per-listing fee; plan $29/month | **$0.00**, and $29 amortised across live listings |
| ZART (we run it) | $0.00, listed as free and unlimited | No set-up fee, no subscription | **$0.00** as published; commission not published, so unverified |

## The real number: cost per listing that actually sells

Take 100 live Etsy listings, auto-renew on: **$80 a year** before a single sale (100 × $0.80). Now spread it over what sells.

| Sell-through (sold ÷ live, 90-day window) | Listings that sold | Dead-listing cost per sale |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | 5 | **$16.00** |
| 15% | 15 | **$5.33** |
| 30% | 30 | **$2.67** |

**Read it against the commission.** On the same $58 order, Etsy's 6.5% is $3.77 (see the [fee comparison guide](/guides/2026-11-04-selling-fee-comparison/)). At 5% sell-through, the catalogue costs **$16.00** - more than four times the fee on that order. Chasing a lower commission while keeping a dead catalogue is rearranging the small number.

## The self-test (two minutes, then every month)

1. Count live listings in the shop dashboard.
2. Count listings that sold in the last 90 days (one sale counts once, not per unit).
3. Sell-through = sold ÷ live. Write the number down with the date.
4. Multiply your per-listing yearly cost by live listings, divide by what sold. That is the number that should make you uncomfortable, not the commission rate.
5. Once a month: retire what has not sold in two cycles, and re-measure. Same window every time, or the trend is noise.

## Our own shop runs the same rule on content

This site measures its catalogue the same way. Today's run scores **32** articles: **18 at 8 or better** (56%), **15 carrying dated fix-by deadlines** (2026-11-11, 11-18, 11-20, 11-25), and **2 deleted and republished** under new addresses when an addendum would have been the cheap way out. Dead inventory costs attention whether it is listings or paragraphs.
