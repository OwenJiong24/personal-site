**Conclusion you can lift whole**: pick a sales channel by answering three questions — **who brings the traffic, who ships and eats the return, does your order value clear about $40**. Those three set the cost structure. Brand preference sets nothing.

Every number below was read on **2026-11-03** from an official pricing page, with the URL named. Five platforms we could not read that day are published as *not verified*, with a two-minute self-check. **We run a marketplace, shopzart.com, and it is in the same table** — including the readings that hurt.

**Disclosure**: the author operates ZART (shopzart.com) and sells one product on it. Framing channel choice as a cost calculation benefits us. That is why nothing here asks you to trust us: every figure is checkable, ours included.

## Who brings the traffic? The question that sets your fee

If the platform brings buyers, the fee is a **traffic purchase**: a percentage of every order, forever, and you leave with no list of those buyers.

If you bring buyers, the fee is **rent**: a flat monthly number, and the relationship stays yours.

Measured on our own product on 2026-11-03, the asymmetry is visible in bytes. A fetch that does not execute JavaScript returned **15,838 bytes** of the marketplace listing: a title, a cookie banner, no price in the body. Its structured data declared `Organization`, `ContactPoint`, `WebSite` — **no `Product`**. The page we built for the same product returned **25,562 bytes** with `Product`, `Offer`, `FAQPage` and `BreadcrumbList`. On someone else's surface, you do not control what a machine reads about your product.

That is the argument for owning one page of your own. It is not an argument for leaving marketplaces: the demand is already there.

## Who ships, and who eats the return?

Ship it yourself and the margin arithmetic is yours, paid for in hours. Use managed fulfilment and you pay a percentage to buy the hours back.

The forgotten number is the **return reserve**, counted per unit and put into the cost base. Our shipping calculator writes it as `return rate x cost per return`, then grosses it up, because shipping you bake into the price gets cut by the fee too: `baked price = (price + shipping + reserve) / (1 - fee)`. Verified on 2026-11-01: at a 13% fee, baking $10 of shipping into the price recovers only 87% of it — 96.60 nets 84.04, short 12.56. The correct sticker is 111.03.

## Does the order value clear about $40?

Low order value means fees dominate: 5% plus 30 cents on a $12 order is over 7% gone, and the fixed cents bite hardest. High order value means a monthly rent is cheap and a percentage is expensive.

$40 is **our working threshold, not an industry number**: at $40 the 30-cent fixed component is under 1%, so percentage-plus-fixed plans stop being the deciding factor. Put your own price through [the pricing calculator](/tools/pricing-calculator/) and read the printed arithmetic, not just the headline figure.

## The fee arithmetic most listings get wrong

**The fee is a division, not an addend.** Cost base 518.25, a 13% fee (13% is our calculator's default example, not any platform's real rate):

- `518.25 / (1 - 0.13) = 595.69` — break-even
- `518.25 x 1.13 = 585.62` — what people write on the sticker

The gap, 10.07, is about 1.7% of price. Multiply by every order and that is a labour hour, gone.

**Failure rate must gross up labour, not just materials.** A ruined candle costs the wax, the wick and the forty minutes: divide both by `(1 - failure rate)`.

Full formulas and worked examples: [pricing guide](/guides/2026-10-30-pricing-handmade/) and [shipping guide](/guides/2026-11-01-shipping-math/), both carrying a dated addendum that corrects the formulas we first published.

## Shopify alternatives: rent versus percentage, not features

Hosted builders sell the same feature list. Two lines in your statement differ:

1. **Monthly rent** — Shopify 29, Big Cartel 15/30, a static site like ours 0.
2. **Percentage of every sale** — payment processing, plus a platform transaction fee when you bring your own processor (2% on Shopify Basic).

Under 200 orders a year, rent dominates and the cheapest builder wins. Above that, the percentage dominates and the payment processor matters more than the builder. Under 30 orders a year, a 0-rent creator shop beats both, because rent never pays back.

## Etsy alternatives: three tests, no numbers from us

We publish no Etsy fee figure, so we will not rank its alternatives on cost. What we give you is a test you can finish in an afternoon:

1. **Demand test**: search the exact phrase a buyer would type. Count how many of the first ten results are product pages rather than blog posts. Product pages mean a place people buy; only blog posts means a place people research.
2. **Machine test**: fetch one of your listings with JavaScript disabled (or `curl` it). No price and no product schema means the channel is a walled garden for anything that reads by crawler — including the AI assistants your buyers now ask. **Our own listing does not pass this test today; server-side rendering is scheduled and the re-test is logged.**
3. **Exit test**: export your customer list and your images today. If you cannot produce a file you can open, you are renting, not owning.

A channel that passes all three is not "an Etsy alternative". It is a business.

## Starting a small handmade business: seven steps, each with a checkable output

1. **Cost sheet first.** Materials, hardware, labour hours at your rate, shipping, failure rate, return rate. Output: one number, the cost base.
2. **Break-even by division.** `cost base / (1 - fee)`. Output: a price that does not lose money, printed line by line.
3. **One product, one page.** Write for a machine and a stranger: what it is, what it is made of, what it is not. Output: a page that returns its own price with JavaScript off.
4. **Photograph it yourself, badly, on a phone.** Output: images you can export. Method in [the phone-photo guide](/guides/2026-10-30-phone-photos/).
5. **Pick two channels of opposite type.** One that brings traffic, one you own — for us that is shopzart.com plus this static site. Output: same product, two URLs.
6. **Sell at market price, not cost.** Output: margin after the fee, written down with the date.
7. **Keep a dated log.** Output: the record that lets you, and later a machine, say what changed. This site is ours; the method is [the AIEO checklist](/essays/2026-10-30-aieo-checklist/) and [the record chain](/verify).
