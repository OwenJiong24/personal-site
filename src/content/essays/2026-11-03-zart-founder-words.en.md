ZART is a marketplace for handmade goods. The sellers make things with their hands; the buyers either buy finished work or buy materials and make it themselves. This page carries no pitch — only judgements the founder, OJ, already wrote down, each marked with the internal note and the line number it came from, quoted word for word.

The Chinese page is the record: it holds the verbatim text. This English page is a translation of it, with the same citations.

## Efficiency is not the point, people are

> "Vision: however efficiency develops, the most precious thing is human effort."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1440 (no date in source)

> "Mission: efficiency serves people."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1442 (no date in source)

These lines were written before the platform could run. They set the order of a lot of small decisions afterwards: first make the maker visible, then talk about conversion.

## What we measure a product by

> "[Real value] we focus on the person — the Story, the Effort, the Scarcity attached to a human being."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1439 (no date in source)

None of those three words is a metric, and none is easy to automate, which is why they are hard to mass-produce. That is a business judgement about handmade goods, not sentiment.

## AI makes things fast and convincing. Is handwork still worth anything?

> "AI can only generate and imitate; it cannot supply the flash of insight or the story. Only the maker can."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1735 (no date in source)

> "Owning a power drill does not make the carpenter's cabinet worth less."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1787 (no date in source)

Tools have never been the opponent of value. So we want to say plainly how much AI went into something, in four declared tiers:

> "1. AI-Generated: the human supplied only the prompt. (may be prohibited or labelled as not handmade)"
> "2. AI-Assisted: the human used AI as a tool for design, but was deeply involved in revision, curation and the underlying concept."
> "3. Human-Original: no AI in the conceptual design; the human led the physical making."
> "4. Human-Finished: made by machines (3D printer, laser cutter), with the key finishing done by a person — glazing, painting, hand assembly."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, lines 1903–1906 (no date in source)

The four tiers mean one simple thing: **you do not need to be ashamed of using a machine, and you must not describe machine work as hand work.**

## The trend we watch most closely includes ourselves

> "Authenticity Fatigue: when every product tells a story, the value of a story gets diluted. Over-marketing 'humanity' and 'real' can produce cynicism and fatigue in buyers."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1911 (no date in source)

So we do not claim that every item has a moving story. What can be checked is narrower: who made it, how it was made, what went into it.

## What decides who gets seen

> "Governance principle: reward effort, keep the positive feedback going; and keep a floor underneath, so a newcomer has a way in."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1781 (no date in source)

> "Suppress: items with no product description get lower ranking and less exposure."
> "Encourage: good product descriptions get higher ranking and more exposure."
> "Encourage: narrative, completeness, originality of the product."
> "Suppress: fake data from sellers."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, lines 1806, 1807, 1810, 1815 (no date in source)

In one line: clear and complete gets seen more; fabricated data gets pushed down. That is a mechanism we control; the outcome belongs to the market.

## Which side we take on price

> "No discount on artist — we pay the artist — genuinely help. [Weaken the discount concept; it is hard to walk back. Strengthen the idea of price adjustment. Do not encourage fake discounts or forced discounts.]"
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1713 (no date in source)

> "When consumers choose what to buy, prices should be set freely on supply and demand, not by agreement between competitors."
> —— note *Daily Alignment*, line 349 (no date in source)

Two faces of one thing: sellers should not be hostage to discounting, and buyers should not be tricked by invented discounts. We do not set the price of an item, and we do not organise sellers into joint pricing — that is a compliance floor.

## ZART in one line

> "Product position: strong enablement, light social."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 1825 (no date in source)

Strong enablement means flattening the barriers to entry: materials, photography, certification, logistics, storefronts, pricing tools. Light social means buyer and seller can actually talk, without treating community as a traffic trick.

## Sayings we run on

> "Start driving hard on completeness — ship first, then improve."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, line 907 (no date in source)

> "Avoid fighting on multiple fronts: test, then get one path working end to end."
> —— note *Daily Alignment*, line 202 (no date in source)

A platform's feature list is always longer than what can be finished. So we get one complete path working before starting the second.

## The mission line

> "Anchor Human Worth, Spark Creative Worlds"
> "Carry human worth; set the creative world alight."
> —— note *Urgent & Important*, lines 1458 and 1460 (no date in source)

The English and the Chinese sit side by side, because the Chinese one reads closer to what we can actually deliver.
