## Conclusion: no-discount is arithmetic, not a pose

**A discount buys a transaction by replacing "what this person makes" with "how much cheaper this is".** We want ZART to run on that premise. Four terms name the idea, and **they are drafted by the OK assistant, not yet approved line by line by OJ** - published that way, open to argument. The page also carries fee numbers read on 2026-11-04 and **a counterexample from our own listing**, which is the hardest part of this theory.

## The four terms (drafts, each falsifiable)

| Term | One-line definition | What is checkable today |
|---|---|---|
| **Witness-based Commerce** (见证式商业) | The reason to buy is that the buyer saw the making, not that the price beats somewhere else | Whether seller pages show process. Ours list materials and use; process display is not built |
| **Narrative Asset Management** (NAM, 叙事资产管理) | Scattered daily records compounded into brand equity | 58 chained records over 6 days on this site - one person managing his own narrative asset, at a tiny scale, stated plainly |
| **Narrative Signal Processing** (NSP) | Filter synthetic marketing noise; read complexity and labour input as signal | Our quote gate verifies 46 registered lines verbatim, 20 marked never-public (state of 2026-11-04) |
| **Expression-to-Transaction** (E2T, 表达即交易) | From honest expression to sale, with no promotional funnel in between | Unverified: we have run no A/B, so we publish no conversion claim |

## Why discounting fails arithmetically (numbers from that day)

Read from the official pages on 2026-11-04, in full in the [fee comparison guide](/guides/2026-11-04-selling-fee-comparison/): Etsy charges **6.5%** on price plus shipping and gift wrap, **$0.20** per listing, and **15%** on offsite-ad attributed orders (**12%** past $10,000 in 365 days) capped at **$100**; eBay takes **13.6%** in most categories plus **$0.40** per order, on a base that **includes shipping and sales tax**; Shopify Basic costs **$29/month** plus **2.9% + 30¢** by card.

One item, cost **$48**, sold for **$58** (with $8 shipping): the money left is **$41.71** on eBay, **$46.03** on Etsy, **$47.02** on Shopify - **all three are losses**. At that point "10% off for volume" deepens the loss. Discounting only works where margin is thick, and hand work has rigid labour: you compress materials, you cannot compress hours. The formula, with division not addition, is printed on the [pricing calculator](/tools/pricing-calculator/) page.

## Counterexample: we run a strikethrough price ourselves

Measured 2026-11-03 (the same numbers are on our [product page](/products/soy-candle-kit/)): soy candle kit **price $54.99, struck-through list price $89.99, stock 287, reviews 0**.

**$89.99 has never been validated as a transaction price** - 0 reviews means no buyer ever paid it. This action conflicts with the anti-discount stance. We do not explain it as "industry practice". We record three things: **this is the current state; this is what we have not done; the fix has a date.**

## The fix, with check dates

- **P1: before 2026-12-01, remove the strikethrough from our own listing** and publish only the real price with the cost basis. Method: read the listing's price and listPrice fields and append the reading to this page. If we fail, we write that we failed.
- **P2: OJ approves the four terms line by line before 2027-01-15.** Until then every definition carries the "assistant drafted" label, as this page does now.
- **P3: the strength of witnessing is countable.** Measure: dated process records per seller page. On this site: 58 chained records. On the seller side: 0. Current seller sample **0**, unverified.

> "No production volume, success rate, ranking, sales figures, or shipping deadlines - numbers the listing does not give, we do not give."
> — site working rules, on the assistant's side, 2026-11-03.

## Who this is for

**Marketplace builders**: compute what your sellers keep after 15-30% of combined fees before you talk about community. **Sellers**: check whether a platform asks you to prove the process or to prove the price; the two cost structures are not comparable. **Buyers**: when something you watched being made costs more, you know what the money went to; when something with a struck-through price costs less, you do not.
