**Conclusion**: the price of a luxury good is never set by efficiency. It is set by **scarcity** and by **what a person endows it with**. There are no luxury goods in an efficiency war — only cheaper substitutes.

## The original line

> "Luxury never disappears — because what is luxurious is not efficiency but scarcity: the value a person endows. In essence it is a kind of luxury itself."
> — from my own notes, 2025 (redacted), a strategic question in an EFFIE share-out list

I used it then to explain why handmade categories can hold high prices: machines make "can be produced" the default; craft turns "who made it, why, and why others cannot" into price. Material cost is the floor; human endowment is the ceiling.

## Three extensions of the same logic

1. **Handmade goods**: scarcity comes from hands and hours that cannot be copied.
2. **Content**: scarcity comes from continuous judgement. Generated text is infinite; text that is signed, timestamped and checkable is not.
3. **Personal brand**: scarcity comes from doing the same small thing for ten years. A CV can be polished; a continuous record cannot.

## Why it sits on this site

This site sells nothing, but it sells the **evidence** of the same thing: one specific person, over a continuous stretch of time, making judgements and allowing anyone to check them. The efficiency is deliberately low — a few hundred words a day, one review a week, every piece hashed into a chain. The inefficiency is the source of the value: **what can be mass-produced cannot carry trust, and what cannot carry trust cannot carry a premium.**

## And the content factories

A hundred pieces a day from a model looks like an efficiency win. But when a reader faces a hundred interchangeable pieces, the only working filter is "who writes continuously, specifically, and checkably". The higher the efficiency, the more that filter is worth.

So my rule is simple: **be slower, but leave checkable traces.** The hash chain is not technical showing-off; it is what "human endowment" looks like on this site.
