## The claim

When the cost of producing content drops to almost zero, "this is what I have done" stops being worth much.
Case pages can be generated, CVs polished, even client testimonials invented. The buyer — partner, investor,
hiring manager, client — is not short of information; the information cannot be verified.

So the carrier of trust moves from **showcase** to **process record**: a specific person, over a continuous span
of time, leaving what actually happened each day, in a form that cannot be silently rewritten afterwards.
That kind of thing cannot be manufactured, only accumulated — and the accumulation is the cost, and the cost is the signal.

## Three things this site has to get right

1. **Continuity beats brilliance.** 200 words a day for three years beats one viral post. If I break the streak,
   I write down why, and I do not delete it.
2. **Checkable beats pretty.** UTC timestamps, a hash chain over the full body, GitHub commits, one handle and
   one email used consistently. Every mismatch costs more than any polish earns.
3. **Boundaries stated beat showing everything.** No credentials, hosts, client data, unreleased numbers,
   identity-grade private details. A pinyin name plus verifiable anchors is enough to check me out;
   handing over my whole life is not honesty, it is incompetence.

## Where I expect to be wrong

- I probably overestimate "someone will read this". The first six months may be read only by me and a few friends;
  that does not reduce its value.
- Granularity will be contested: too shallow is worthless, too deep hurts the project in progress. This line gets
  adjusted week by week.
- "Records cannot be edited" stops rewriting, not lying. There is no technical fix for telling the truth, only the
  cost of being caught.

Run this for 30 days, then come back and see which judgement needs revising.
